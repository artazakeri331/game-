    Diplomacy

    How to play

    • Move the map: drag it, or use WASD or the arrow keys. Scroll or pinch to zoom. Click the minimap to jump.
    • The map is the real Earth, one tile about 33 km wide. Pick a country, then click land to send troops. Grey land is unclaimed (small countries and wilderness) and is cheap to take. Enemy land costs more the denser their troops. Islands and coasts across a narrow strait (about 230 km) can still be attacked by land.
    • Troops regrow up to a cap set by your land, cities and barracks. Gold comes from land and cities.
    • Make soldiers faster: build barracks for steady troops, recruit troops instantly with gold in the Troops tab, or run a conscription drive for triple growth.
    • All-in button (or X): turns on one-click spending. With it on, one click on land builds as many of the selected building as your gold allows around that spot, one click on a building upgrades its whole type, and Recruit uses all your gold. SAM sites are easy to fool at level 1 and get much harder to beat at level 4.
    • Satellites (Air tab, up to 3) are your spy network. Without them other nations' troop numbers show as ?. Level 1 shows rough troops, level 2 exact troops, gold and buildings, level 3 their war plans, who they fight, and alerts when they prepare or launch an attack on you.
    • Retaking your own land is cheaper: your homeland tiles cost about a third as many troops to recapture, and a nation that has lost land keeps part of its old troop cap so it can fight back.
    • Battles are decided by force. Sending a bigger army than the defenders have makes you faster and cheaper per tile; sending less makes you slower and the AI wins ground. The defenders lose troops for every tile you take, and troops you send out also still help defend home. Your homeland tiles are cheaper to retake.
    • Friends are hard to make now: trade deals need real goodwill (gifts give only +5 and work every 45s, and stop helping once a nation already likes you). Alliances need 90 seconds of trade, a very warm relationship, and a clean reputation, and you can have at most 3 allies. Open disputes block deals until you settle them.
    • Arsenal tab: buy missiles ahead of time and keep them in your silos (each silo level holds more). Stored missiles launch for free from the Missiles tab, so you can fire the moment you want to. If you have none stored, launching still buys one on the spot. Destroyed silos lose their stored missiles.
    • Doomsday missile (500,000 gold, needs 3 silos): cannot be shot down and destroys a huge area, about a quarter of a big country. Everyone hates you for it.
    • Nuclear missiles: Nuclear (320 gold, one silo, radius 6) and Thermonuclear (650 gold, two silos, radius 9). Using either makes you infamous and other nations like you less. A nuclear deal in Diplomacy forbids them.
    • Ships: Coastal batteries (Navy tab) shoot enemy troop ships nearby, ports shoot a little, and airfields with fighters also fire at ships close by. Navy > Sink ship fires an anti-ship missile at a ship you click. A sunk ship loses every soldier on it.
    • Fighter jets guard your airfields. They cannot be flown by hand: they automatically shoot at enemy bombers and missiles passing within about 34 tiles (only from nations at war with you), and at enemy troop ships nearby.
    • Combat: every tile costs about as many troops as the defender has per tile (mountains cost nearly three times more) and the defender loses almost as many. In Attack mode with satellite level 2, hover enemy land to see how many tiles your troops can take. To beat a nation you usually need clearly more troops than it has, and the Troops slider decides how many you commit.
    • Diplomacy: Settle dispute (gold) removes a rivalry such as the Iran and USA sanctions feud and warms relations. Nuclear deal bans nuclear strikes between you and the other nation and calms tensions. When someone attacks your ally you get a call to arms: join the war or stay out.
    • Build cities, barracks, markets, mines, missile silos, SAM sites (they shoot missiles down), airfields for jets, defense posts and ports. Use Upgrade in the Build tab and click a building to raise it up to level 4.
    • No land border? Build a Port on your coast, then use the Navy tab: click an enemy (at war) or unclaimed coast and a transport fleet sails there, lands a beachhead and keeps fighting. More and higher-level ports carry bigger landings.
    • Missiles flatten land and kill troops in a blast circle. MIRVs overwhelm SAMs. Thermonuclear warheads need two silos.
    • Bombers fly out, hit a target and fly home to be reused. Fighters guard and shoot down missiles and bombers.
    • AI nations only go to war for a reason: a real-world dispute (Kashmir, Taiwan, Crimea, the Korean border and more), a border feud, revenge, a rising superpower, or to defend an ally. The reason shows in the news. Wars against a rival you have a dispute with cost you no reputation.
    • You are a president, not just a general. You cannot attack a nation unless you are at war with it: open Diplomacy (P) to declare war, ask for a ceasefire, sign trade deals, form alliances or send gifts. The game pauses while you negotiate.
    • Run the economy in the Economy tab. Taxes raise gold but lower approval; unhappy people grow troops slower and revolt if approval collapses. Build markets and mines, sign trade deals, or issue bonds for quick cash.
    • Decisions pop up now and then (strikes, investors, scandals) and AI nations send offers. Answer them before the timer runs out.
    • Win by holding 50% of the land, by being the last nation standing, or by leading an alliance bloc that holds 55% of the land for 45 seconds.
